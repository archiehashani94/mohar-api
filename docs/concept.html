Insurers pay. Diagnoses stay sealed.
Cashless health claims settled in under a minute. The insurer never learns what was wrong with you, and the payment doesn't give it away either.

The problem

To get paid, you hand over your medical history

Today, a hospital that wants an insurer to settle a cashless claim sends the diagnosis, discharge summary, prescriptions and bills. Then a person at the insurer reads all of it and decides by hand.

Privacy

Your diagnosis, mental-health history and reproductive care all end up in an insurer's database you have no control over.

Hours

The insurance regulator (IRDAI) requires a cashless claim to be approved within 3 hours of the discharge request. Patients still wait at the billing desk for half a day.

Fraud

Fake hospitals, claims for patients who were never treated, the same claim filed twice, and big bills split up to slip under limits.

The insight that sets Kavach apart

Most ZK claim systems still leak the diagnosis

A typical submission proves the claim is valid without revealing the diagnosis, then has the smart contract pay the hospital's public wallet. That's where the privacy breaks.

The payment is the leak

If the money lands in the public wallet of a cancer centre, an IVF clinic, an HIV clinic or a psychiatric hospital, anyone reading the chain can work out the diagnosis. The ZK proof hid it, then the payment revealed it.

Kavach closes it: Ghost Pay on Zcash

The proof is checked on an EVM smart contract, but the payout goes to the hospital's Zcash shielded address, with the claim's ID in the encrypted memo. The amount the insurer agreed to is public; who received it is not. It stays private from the hospital's computer all the way to the payout.

How it works

One claim, start to finish

Everything inside the dashed box stays on the hospital's computer. Only a proof and a few public numbers ever leave it.

1

Patient consents

At discharge the patient scans a QR code in the Vault app and signs the claim. Without that signature, the proof can't be generated.

2

Hospital generates the proof locally

The Seal desk builds a zero-knowledge proof in the browser from the diagnosis, procedure code, policy details and the hospital's secret key. None of that data is uploaded.

3

Contract verifies

The proof plus a few public numbers go on-chain. The contract checks the proof, checks the claim's nullifier hasn't been used (blocking replays), checks the remaining policy limit and checks the date.

4

Insurer sees only checkmarks

The Blind Desk shows the amount and five green checks. Nobody at the insurer approves anything; the payment goes out on its own.

5

Paid privately

Ghost Pay sends the money to the hospital's Zcash shielded address. Nobody watching the chain can tell which hospital was paid.

The product

Five parts

Three dashboards (one per role), a private payout layer, and a live demo that shows attacks failing.

🗝

Vault Patient

  • Health records encrypted and stored on IPFS in FHIR format; only the hash goes on-chain
  • Grant a doctor access with a timer (e.g. 24h); revoke anytime
  • Every view and change is logged on-chain
  • Break-glass: 2 of your 3 chosen guardians can unlock records in an emergency, logged loudly, and you're notified
  • Approve claims by scanning a QR code
🏥

Seal Hospital

  • Fill in the claim: procedure, amount, policy
  • Generates the proof in the browser, with a live timer
  • Hospital proves it's licensed by using its secret key, so it can't pretend to be another hospital
  • Requests access to a patient's records from the patient
👁

Blind Desk Insurer

  • Issues policies: only a fingerprint (commitment) of each policy goes on-chain
  • Updates the list of approved hospitals (published as a Merkle root)
  • A claims feed showing only the amount and green checks
  • Funds the payout pool; claims settle automatically
👻

Ghost Pay Zcash

  • When the contract emits ClaimApproved, the payout goes to the hospital's Zcash shielded address
  • The claim's nullifier goes in the encrypted memo, so the hospital can match payment to claim
  • Fallback: one-time stealth addresses on the EVM chain (ERC-5564)

Known trade-off, stated openly: the insurer can see how many claims hit each policy, since it has to track the limit. Roadmap fix: hide policies in a Merkle tree and track spending through nullifiers.
